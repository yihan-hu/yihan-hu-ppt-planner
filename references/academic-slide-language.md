# Academic Slide Language

## Purpose

Keep visible slide wording consistent with conventional scientific presentations. The planning logic can be claim-driven, but the audience-facing deck should usually read like an academic talk rather than a pitch deck, editorial, or internal design memo.

## 1. Default title grammar

Use short, neutral, descriptive titles or noun phrases by default. Common forms include:

- `Background`
- `Previous studies`
- `Study rationale`
- `Study overview`
- `Data sources`
- `Study population`
- `Eligibility criteria`
- `Study design`
- `Sequential target trial emulation`
- `Statistical analysis`
- `Covariate balance`
- `Primary results`
- `Sensitivity analyses`
- `Secondary analyses`
- `Design 3: two-state analysis`
- `Design 3: three-state analysis`
- `Discussion`
- `Strengths and limitations`
- `Conclusion`

A short qualifier after a colon is acceptable when it identifies the analysis, population, or outcome.

## 2. Separate title from interpretation

Do not make the title carry the slide's conclusion by default. A result slide titled `Primary results` may show HRs near 1.0 without being titled `Both target trials were null`. A treatment-state slide may be titled `Design 3: three-state analysis` without being titled `Discontinuation explains the protective effect`.

Place interpretation in one of these locations instead:

- the figure or table itself;
- a concise objective caption;
- a short result sentence below the figure;
- the spoken narrative;
- a dedicated `Discussion` or `Interpretation` slide;
- the final `Conclusion`.

## 3. Methods wording

Methods slides should describe what was done, not advertise why it is superior. Prefer:

- `Clone-censor-weighting with stabilized IPCW`
- `IPCW model included prespecified baseline covariate domains`
- `Weighted marginal survival estimates`
- `Within-individual fixed-effects Cox model`

Avoid:

- `Why our design is better`
- `A more rigorous approach`
- `The point is reproducibility, not implementation code`
- `Our key methodological advantage`

If the scientific contribution is methodological, explain the rationale objectively in the body or spoken narrative.

## 4. Results wording

Results slides should describe what was observed. Prefer neutral captions such as:

- `Hazard-ratio estimates were close to 1.0 across both target-trial populations.`
- `Current-use estimates were close to 1.0 after post-discontinuation time was separated.`
- `Post-discontinuation periods showed higher observed risk.`

Avoid language that overstates causality or certainty, such as:

- `The model proves that discontinuation caused the risk.`
- `The protective effect was false.`
- `This analysis explains everything.`

## 5. Planner language must stay off slides

The following are internal planning concepts and should not be copied into visible slide text unless the user explicitly requests them:

- `scientific job`
- `narrative beat`
- `selling point`
- `payoff`
- `tension`
- `the point is...`
- `this slide should convince the audience...`
- instructions to the presenter
- comments about why a slide was included

Use these concepts to plan the deck, not to write the deck.

## 6. When claim titles are acceptable

Use declarative or claim-style titles only when at least one is true:

- the user explicitly asks for claim/headline titles;
- the talk is intentionally keynote/editorial/consulting style rather than a conventional academic presentation;
- a single synthesis or conclusion slide benefits from a concise statement;
- the source presentation already uses that convention and the user wants it preserved.

Even then, keep claims scientifically calibrated and source-supported.

## 7. Audit

Before finalizing a storyboard, ask:

- Would these titles look normal in a clinical/epidemiology seminar or conference talk?
- Are titles describing slide content rather than selling an interpretation?
- Has any internal planning note leaked into visible slide copy?
- Are methods phrased as procedures and results phrased as observations?
- Is interpretation concentrated in Discussion/Conclusion rather than spread across every title?
